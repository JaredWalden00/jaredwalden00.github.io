VR / AR Analysis

Decentraland and the Metaverse

For this project, I looked at Decentraland as an example of a modern attempt at creating a metaverse. The idea behind it is that people can exist in a virtual world, own land, interact socially, and treat digital space almost like real life.

First Impression

When I first looked at Decentraland, it presents itself as this large, open virtual world where you can explore and interact. But once you actually get into it, it feels pretty empty. There isn’t much to do besides walk around.

One of the most basic interactions is just moving and jumping, which is kind of surprising considering how long the platform has been in development.

The world looks open and interactive, but lacks meaningful interaction.

Limited Interaction

A big thing I noticed is how limited the actual gameplay is. There aren’t really systems that keep you engaged. Even something as basic as jumping was added years after development started.

That shows that the focus wasn’t really on building a strong experience first, but more on the idea of the world itself.

Basic mechanics like movement are present, but depth is lacking.

The “Magic Circle” Concept

This connects to the idea of the “magic circle,” where people agree to treat a space as meaningful under a shared set of rules. Like in a game, we all agree what counts and what doesn’t.

Decentraland depends heavily on this idea. It expects users to believe that virtual land has value, that digital items matter, and that this space could become a real alternative to everyday life.

The platform relies on users buying into the idea of a meaningful virtual world.

The Core Problem

The issue is that the platform was built around selling land before there was a strong reason for people to actually be there. It feels like the value is assumed rather than earned.

Because of that, it comes across less like a living world and more like something built on speculation.

Virtual land becomes the focus, rather than meaningful interaction.
The platform assumes people will move into the metaverse, but it doesn’t fully justify why they would.

Final Thoughts

Overall, Decentraland is interesting as an idea, but it doesn’t fully deliver as an experience. It relies more on people believing in the future of the metaverse than on what it currently offers.

That’s really the main takeaway I had from looking into it.

The concept is strong, but the execution feels incomplete.
