VR/AR in Sci-Fi: Outer Wilds – Echoes of the Eye

For this assignment, I chose Outer Wilds: Echoes of the Eye, the DLC to Outer Wilds. I had played the original game before and recently experienced the DLC. The game is both awe-inspiring and terrifying, presenting a universe on the brink of collapse as stars begin to explode. Within this setting, a mysterious species known as the Strangers use a form of virtual reality to escape their fate, which is what I will focus on.

The Strangers and Their Technology

The Strangers are bird-reptile-like beings who arrived in the solar system after detecting signals from the Eye of the Universe. They initially viewed it almost like a deity. However, after witnessing what the Eye truly represents, they become deeply afraid. Without going into spoilers, their vision suggests something massive and existentially unsettling, almost Lovecraftian in nature.

Instead of confronting this reality, the Strangers create a new technology centered around a lamp.

How the VR Experience Works

The process of entering their virtual world is simple but symbolic. They sit by a fire holding an unlit lamp and fall asleep. When they enter the virtual world, the lamp becomes lit.

This lamp acts as both the gateway and their safety. Inside the virtual world, it is essential for survival. Losing it results in death within that space.

What makes this system especially interesting is that it goes beyond physical death. Even after their real bodies have died, the Strangers continue to exist inside the simulation. They have essentially abandoned reality and chosen to live indefinitely in a constructed world.

They also block signals from the Eye of the Universe, fully isolating themselves from the truth they fear.

Breaking the Illusion

As the player explores this virtual world, it appears fully immersive and real at first. Over time, you discover ways to break the simulation and see it for what it actually is. It becomes clear that the world is artificial, structured, and ultimately just code.

The lamp represents safety, but also denial. The simulation provides comfort, but is rooted in fear. Letting go of the illusion forces both the player and the Strangers to confront reality and death.

Analysis

Is This Technology Plausible?

Rating: 1 out of 5

This type of VR, where consciousness is fully transferred into a digital world even after death, is far beyond anything we can currently achieve. While brain-computer interfaces are being researched, nothing comes close to the level shown in the game.

Effectiveness in the Story

Rating: 5 out of 5

Within the story, the technology is extremely effective. It allows an entire civilization to escape fear, avoid confronting reality, and continue existing indefinitely. At the same time, it leads to isolation and stagnation.

Final Thoughts

Even though the technology itself is unrealistic, the motivation behind it feels very real. The Strangers create their virtual world because they are afraid of death and the unknown.

This connects to how VR is used in our world today. While we are not escaping the end of the universe, we do use VR to avoid risk, simulate experiences, and temporarily escape reality.

The game ultimately asks whether it is better to live in a safe illusion or face reality, even if it is uncertain and frightening.

To reach the ending, the player must move past the artificial world and confront what lies beyond it. The message suggests that meaning comes from engaging with reality rather than avoiding it.
